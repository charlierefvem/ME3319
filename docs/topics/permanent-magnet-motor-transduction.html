Magnetic Flux, Faraday's Law, and Back-EMF

The flux through one turn of the coil is

𝜙=∫𝑆⃗𝐵⋅𝑑⃗𝑆.

Assume the magnetic field is uniform:

𝜙=⃗𝐵⋅∫𝑆𝑑⃗𝑆.

Assume the coil is flat:

𝜙=⃗𝐵⋅⃗𝑆.

Using the definition of the dot product,

𝜙=‖⃗𝐵‖‖⃗𝑆‖cos⁡𝜃=𝐵𝐴cos⁡𝜃.

The flux linkage through all 𝑁 turns is

𝜆=𝑁𝜙,𝜆=𝑁𝐵𝐴cos⁡𝜃.

For a rectangular coil,

𝜆=2𝑁𝐵𝑟ℓcos⁡𝜃.
Figure


Rectangular coil dimensions.

Faraday's law gives

E=𝑑𝜆𝑑𝑡.

Usually Faraday's law includes a negative sign to show that the induced EMF is a back-EMF, consistent with Lenz's law. With voltage drop already treated as positive in the ME322 convention, that additional negative sign is unnecessary.

The back-EMF is

E=−2𝑁𝐵𝑟ℓsin⁡𝜃Ω,

with back-EMF coefficient

𝐾𝑣(𝜃)=−2𝑁𝐵𝑟ℓsin⁡𝜃.

Lorentz Force and Torque Generation

Figure


Force and moment references about the motor axis.

The Lorentz force law gives the force on each side:

⃗𝐹(−)=𝑖(⃗𝐿(−)×⃗𝐵)=𝑖(ℓˆ𝑘×(−𝐵ˆ𝑗)),⃗𝐹(−)=𝐵𝑖ℓˆ𝑖,
⃗𝐹(+)=𝑖(⃗𝐿(+)×⃗𝐵)=𝑖(−ℓˆ𝑘×(−𝐵ˆ𝑗)),⃗𝐹(+)=−𝐵𝑖ℓˆ𝑖.

Sum moments about the motor axis:

𝑇ˆ𝑘+𝑁(⃗𝑟(−)×⃗𝐹(−)+⃗𝑟(+)×⃗𝐹(+))=0.

Therefore,

𝑇ˆ𝑘=−𝑁[(𝑟cos⁡𝜃ˆ𝑖+𝑟sin⁡𝜃ˆ𝑗)×(𝐵𝑖ℓˆ𝑖)+(𝑟cos⁡𝜃ˆ𝑖−𝑟sin⁡𝜃ˆ𝑗)×(−𝐵𝑖ℓˆ𝑖)],𝑇ˆ𝑘=𝑁(𝑟sin⁡𝜃𝐵𝑖ℓ+𝑟sin⁡𝜃𝐵𝑖ℓ)ˆ𝑘.
𝑇=2𝑁𝐵𝑟ℓsin⁡𝜃𝑖.

This is the same coefficient 𝐾𝑣(𝜃).

Brushes and Commutation

Brushes provide rotational contact between the rotor and stator to conduct current across the rotating boundary. Along with the commutator, they provide:

  • Electrical commutation, or switching of current, to provide DC torque output.
  • Rectification of back-EMF to provide DC output voltage.
Figure


Commutation rectifies the alternating profile.

The back-EMF and torque profiles, defined by the dynamic coefficient

𝐾𝑣(𝜃)=−2𝑁𝐵ℓ𝑅sin⁡𝜃,

are alternating waveforms rather than constant. Consequently, for constant current the torque output is sinusoidal, and for constant velocity the back-EMF is sinusoidal. The model so far is a PMAC motor model rather than a PMDC motor model.

Commutation is needed to rectify these waveforms. As the brushes pass along commutator segments, the constant input current alternates in phase with the torque profile, always producing positive torque. Commutation also makes the output voltage, or motor back-EMF, always positive.

Multiphase Commutation

Figure


Current paths through a multiphase winding.

Current enters the positive brush and splits between commutator segments 1 and 7. Half goes clockwise from 1 →4, and the other half goes counterclockwise from 7 →4. The resultant 𝐵coil is always close to horizontal and therefore perpendicular to 𝐵PM, maximizing torque output.

Figure


Multiphase commutation reduces torque ripple.

At constant current,

𝑇=𝐾𝑇(𝜃)𝑖,𝑇≈2𝜋𝐾𝑇(90∘)𝑖.

As the number of poles approaches infinity, the torque approaches

𝑇=2𝜋𝐾𝑇(90∘)𝑖.
